Reading notes

FY2025 results and the three-year arc
Why FY2025 net income exceeds pre-tax income

Taxes added $1.4B instead of taking it away

FY2025 income taxes were a $1,428M benefit (effective rate −11.9%). When Disney completed the NBCU buyout, Hulu's U.S. income tax classification changed and produced a non-cash benefit of about $3.3B. FY2024 carried a $1,796M expense at a 23.7% rate; FY2023, $1,379M at 28.9%.

Where the profit comes from

Experiences is 38% of revenue and 57% of segment profit

Parks, cruises and consumer products earned $9,995M of the $17,551M in FY2025 segment operating income, at a 27.6% margin that has held near 27% for all three years. From Q1 FY2027 Disney moves Consumer Products licensing and publishing into Entertainment; switch to the FY2027 structure to see Experiences' margin fall toward 25% and Entertainment's rise toward 14% with no change to the consolidated P&L.

The three-year arc

Net income to Disney went $2.4B → $5.0B → $12.4B

Restructuring and impairment charges fell from $3.9B to $3.6B to $0.8B. Direct-to-Consumer operating income went from a $2.5B loss to $143M to $1.3B while Linear Networks slid from $4.1B to $3.0B. Revenue grew only 6% over the two years; the profit recovery came from below the revenue line. The line-profitability view shows it business by business.

How this chart defines its layers

Gross profit and operating income are derived, not reported

Disney's statement has no gross-profit line. Here gross profit is revenue less cost of services and products, and operating income is gross profit less SG&A, depreciation and amortization, and restructuring. Equity income from investees, other income and tax benefits enter as separate additions; the segment views use the costs Disney reports for each segment.
